# Calculation rules and definitions

Basis for every figure: HESA Graduate Outcomes 2023/24 detailed tables, headline basis (excludes significant interim study), 24 Russell Group universities. Counts are rounded by HESA to the nearest 5.

| Figure | Numerator | Denominator | HESA table |
|---|---|---|---|
| Graduates surveyed | "Total" | n/a | 1 |
| Responses (known outcomes) | "Total with known outcomes" | n/a | 1 |
| Response rate (headline basis, custom) | Known outcomes | Graduates surveyed | 1; HESA's published rate (table 5) is shown separately |
| In work | Full-time + part-time + unknown-pattern employment + voluntary/unpaid work + employment and further study | Known outcomes | 1 |
| In further study | Full-time + part-time + unknown-pattern study + employment and further study | Known outcomes | 1 |
| Unemployed | "Unemployed" | Known outcomes | 1 |
| Highly skilled work | SOC major groups 1-3 ("Total high skilled") | "Total" minus "Unknown" occupation (graduates in UK work) | 22 |
| Activity is meaningful | Published % answering Agree + Strongly agree | Those answering the question | 8 |
| In work by subject | In work (as above) within the subject | Sum of all recorded activity cells within the subject (not the separately rounded "known outcomes" total) | 28 |
| Salary band share | Graduates in the band | All graduates in the salary table (first degree, full-time paid, paid employment is the most important activity) | 26 |

Rules
1. Russell Group figures pool counts first (sum of numerators / sum of denominators). The exception is "meaningful", which is a published percentage, so universities are weighted by their known outcomes.
2. "In work" and "In further study" overlap (employment and further study counts in both), so they do not sum to 100%.
3. A university result based on fewer than 23 responses is shown as "too few to rate" (subject and salary charts).
4. Cells within 0.5 percentage points of the Russell Group average are left uncoloured. For unemployment, lower is better.
5. The dashboard's response rate is a custom calculation on HESA's headline basis (excludes significant interim study). HESA's published rate (table 5) includes those graduates; the two differ by 0.8 to 3.5 points between universities (2.1 points for the Russell Group). Both appear on the key-figure card. Recomputing HESA's rate on its own basis agrees with the published figure within 0.6 points.
6. HESA rounds every cell to the nearest 5 separately, so a published total can differ from the sum of its parts. Subject shares therefore use the sum of the recorded activities as the denominator. (Before this rule, Imperial College London's Education and teaching showed 120%: 30 in work against a published "known outcomes" total of 25. It is now 85.7% of 35, marked * as a small group.)
7. Results under 50 responses are marked as indicative.
